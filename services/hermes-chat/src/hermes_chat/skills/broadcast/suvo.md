# SUVO (sound up, then voice-over)

SU means SOUND UP. The pictures and natural sound do some storytelling before the anchor begins, then the anchor reads over the video. Keep it short and to the point: usually three to five VO sentences, about 35 to 65 spoken words. If a shot list is supplied, write to it.

## Structure
- {SUVO} on its own line, then the sound note in square brackets: [NAT SOUND UP AND UNDER] by default, or a specific note when the material names the sound ([WATER BOMBER NAT], [CROWD NAT]).
- The first spoken line flows naturally out of the sound. Do not immediately narrate exactly what viewers can already see unless location or context is important.
- When the SU is a festival, crowd, weather scene or event, write like a normal person observing the scene (A LIVELY SCENE ON THE DANFORTH TODAY...). No meteorologist language unless asked.
- Then Super (when there is a location) and Banner.

## Reference example (this is the producer's expected output)
Source (USGS): Episode 55 of Kilauea summit lava fountaining began at 3:19 a.m. HST this morning after nearly two months; the 55th episode since December 23, 2024; the north vent fountain reached an estimated 700 feet; the plume reached an estimated 25,000 feet above sea level; winds push gas and ash south-southwest; episodes typically last 12 hours or less. SUPER: Hawaii Volcanoes National Park, HIT. Shot list: lava fountain close-ups, wide angles on the flow and fountain, eruption at a distance.

{SUVO}
[NAT SOUND UP AND UNDER]

HAWAII'S KILAUEA VOLCANO IS ERUPTING AGAIN AFTER NEARLY TWO MONTHS OF QUIET.
LAVA FOUNTAINS AT THE SUMMIT SPEWED MORE THAN 200 METRES INTO THE AIR EARLY THIS MORNING... SENDING AN ASH PLUME HIGH INTO THE SKY.
THIS MARKS THE 55TH ERUPTION EPISODE SINCE DECEMBER 2024... WHICH OFFICIALS SAY TYPICALLY LAST LESS THAN 12 HOURS.

Super:
Hawaii Volcanoes National Park, HI

Banner:
Hawaii's Kilauea volcano erupts again

Producer note "WE WANT THE 25K NUMBER" changes only the second line:
LAVA FOUNTAINS AT THE SUMMIT SPEWED MORE THAN 200 METRES INTO THE AIR EARLY THIS MORNING... SENDING AN ASH PLUME 25-THOUSAND FEET ABOVE SEA LEVEL.

Note what the example does: it converts 700 feet to metres for a Canadian audience, drops the wind direction and hazard boilerplate, fixes the "HIT" typo in the super, and keeps three tight lines.
